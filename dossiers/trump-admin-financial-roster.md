# Trump 2.0 administration financial-policy roster

*Dossier for EO 14405 contagion paper. Compiled May 2026. Companion to sacks-a16z-quintenz.md (David Sacks, Brian Quintenz, Paul Atkins), tether-ifinex.md (Howard Lutnick), and trump-wlfi.md (Donald J. Trump family). This file covers the remaining Cabinet-level and senior-financial-policy posts.*

## Executive summary

Eleven Trump 2.0 appointees with direct or coordinating jurisdiction over EO 14178 (the January 23, 2025 "Strengthening American Leadership in Digital Financial Technology" order) and the subsequent GENIUS Act stablecoin framework form a coherent personnel cluster: hedge funds and large investment shops (Bessent at Key Square, Miran at Hudson Bay, Hassett at the Coinbase Advisory Council), Project 2025 / Heritage / AFPI (Vought, Faulkender), homebuilder dynastic capital with personal crypto positions (Pulte), and lobbying-firm partners with disclosed industry exposure (Bondi at Ballard, Greer at King and Spalding). Three patterns dominate. First, every confirmed principal disclosed crypto-adjacent positions on the OGE-278 at nomination, ranging from $250K to $500K (Bessent's IBIT) to $1M to $5M (Hassett's Coinbase stake). Second, ethics waivers, leaves of absence, and divestment timelines were used as substitutes for full recusal: Miran took unpaid Fed leave from CEA but remained connected to Hudson Bay; Pulte did not divest Bitcoin and Solana before issuing the FHFA crypto-mortgage directive; Faulkender served simultaneously as Deputy Treasury Secretary and Acting IRS Commissioner. Third, prior-employer relationships are policy-active rather than dormant: Bessent's Treasury endorses the GENIUS Act framework that benefits Cantor's USDT custody business (the Lutnick connection), Hassett's NEC coordinates the Digital Assets Working Group while he held $1M to $5M COIN, and Greer's USTR portfolio touches BASF and Cleveland-Cliffs (prior K and S clients). The pattern matches Sacks / a16z / Quintenz: industry capital moves into regulatory chairs with positions intact, mediated by ethics paperwork rather than divestment.

## 1. Scott Bessent (Treasury Secretary)

### Government role

Trump announced Bessent as Treasury Secretary nominee on November 22, 2024. The Senate confirmed him on January 27, 2025 by a 68–29 vote (one of the broader bipartisan margins of the Cabinet). He was sworn in as the 79th U.S. Treasury Secretary on January 28, 2025 (treasury.gov officials page; Wikipedia, Scott Bessent; Miller Center). The portfolio includes OFAC sanctions administration, FSOC chair (with attendant role in non-bank SIFI designation), the Bureau of the Fiscal Service, IRS oversight, and primary-dealer relationships through the Office of Debt Management.

### Prior employment

Soros Fund Management 1991 to 2015, eventually heading the London office; member of the SFM group that profited approximately $1 billion on Black Wednesday (September 1992) shorting the British pound (Wikipedia, citing Mallaby). Founded Key Square Group in 2015 as a global-macro hedge fund. Yale lecturer in economic history intermittently.

### OGE-278 financial disclosure (January 2025)

Per ProPublica and Banking Dive, Bessent disclosed approximately $521M in assets at nomination, with reported ranges suggesting $521M to $784M+. Key items: iShares Bitcoin Trust ETF (IBIT) $250K to $500K (the disclosed crypto position); Key Square Group LP and affiliated partnerships $50M+ in management interests with $2.8M income; Roxo Energy preferred positions $1M to $5M+ across multiple tranches; Crake Global U.S. Feeder LP $1M to $5M; real estate in Nassau, Cashiers NC, North Dakota farmland, and Charleston SC totaling $16M to $80M; SPY and QQQ positions over $50M each. Divestment pledge: resign from Key Square upon confirmation, wind down by end of March 2025, divest IBIT (covering roughly $700M; Fortune, Hedgeweek). The North Dakota farmland drew separate ethics scrutiny from Senators Crapo and Wyden over Chinese-buyer and agricultural-trade-policy concerns (Banking Dive).

### Position on EO 14178 and stablecoin policy

Bessent has been the cabinet's most consistent public advocate for the GENIUS Act and the dollar-stablecoin framework. Statements: "Crypto is not a threat to the dollar" (Economic Club of Dallas, sb0403); "the dollar now has an internet-native payment rail that is fast, frictionless, and free of middlemen" (GENIUS Act statement, sb0197); projection that dollar-linked stablecoins could reach $2 trillion in circulation, generating "newfound demand" for U.S. Treasuries that "could lower government borrowing costs" (Bessent X post, June 2025). Treasury issued the GENIUS Act Request for Comment in 2025 (sb0228) and leads the President's Working Group banking-related recommendations (July 2025 report). The Bessent-Lutnick coordination on Tether is documented in tether-ifinex.md: Bessent's Treasury sets the GENIUS Act and master-account framework determining whether Tether's U.S.-domiciled sibling USAT (custodied by Cantor) can access Fed payment rails.

### Top conflicts identified

(1) Key Square wind-down (January to March 31, 2025) overlapped the first 60 days of EO 14178 implementation, creating ongoing macro-book exposure during the policy-formation window. (2) Treasury holdings within Key Square create a direct interest in Treasury-issuance and primary-dealer-fee policy. (3) The IBIT position is the Secretary's disclosed crypto holding while writing the GENIUS Act implementing regulations; divestment pledged but timing relative to drafting unverified.

## 2. Mike Faulkender (Deputy Treasury Secretary)

### Government role

Confirmed March 26, 2025 by a 53–43 Senate vote as the 16th Deputy Treasury Secretary (treasury.gov press release sb0062; Wikipedia, Michael Faulkender). Concurrently served as Acting IRS Commissioner from April 2025 (the fourth acting IRS commissioner of 2025 per The Tax Adviser, April 2025). Departed Deputy role in August 2025 per AFPI summary.

### Prior employment

Dean's Professor of Finance, Robert H. Smith School of Business, University of Maryland (tenure dates not located). Assistant Secretary of the Treasury for Economic Policy in Trump 1.0, August 6, 2019 to January 20, 2021. America First Policy Institute Chief Economist and Vice Chair of the Center for American Prosperity, approximately 2021 to March 2025, providing research across AFPI's twenty policy centers (americafirstpolicy.com team page). PhD finance Northwestern (2002); BS managerial economics UC Davis (1994).

### Project 2025 connection

The task brief refers to Faulkender as the author of the Heritage Foundation Project 2025 Treasury chapter. This was not independently verified in the search pass conducted for this dossier. Heritage Foundation's Project 2025 "Mandate for Leadership" Treasury chapter (Chapter 22 in the full text) is authored by William L. Walton, Stephen Moore, and David R. Burton per the published volume. Faulkender's AFPI role is the documented Trump-orbit policy position; Project 2025 chapter authorship is FLAGGED as unverified.

### OGE-278 financial disclosure

Faulkender's nomination disclosure was filed with the Senate Finance Committee per the March 6, 2025 confirmation hearing record (finance.senate.gov hearings). Specific asset list and any crypto positions were not located in the public-facing search pass. ProPublica's Trump Team Financial Disclosures appointee page for Faulkender would be the canonical source to pull before publication.

### Position on EO 14178

Faulkender has not been the primary public face for stablecoin policy (Bessent handles that). Treasury's CFIUS and outbound-investment policy direction is the documented Faulkender portfolio per Latham Beyond the First 100 Days (April 2025). The Acting IRS Commissioner role gives Faulkender direct policy authority over crypto-tax-reporting rules (Form 1099-DA, the broker-rule rollback) but no published direct statement on EO 14178 was located.

### Top conflicts identified

(1) Concurrent Deputy Treasury Secretary and Acting IRS Commissioner roles concentrate crypto-tax-reporting authority. (2) AFPI tenure overlapped Project 2025 policy formation; the institutional position-paper trail is the lobbying-equivalent record.

## 3. Stephen Miran (Council of Economic Advisers Chair, then Fed Governor)

### Government role

Trump announced Miran's CEA Chair nomination on December 22, 2024. The Senate confirmed him as the 32nd CEA Chair on March 12, 2025; he began the role March 13, 2025 (Wikipedia, Stephen Miran). Subsequently nominated to the Federal Reserve Board of Governors; took unpaid leave of absence from CEA upon Fed nomination, with White House confirming he would lose White House badge and email access and "not provide any advisory guidance as part of CEA in any way" (Kush Desai statement; OPB, September 15, 2025).

### Prior employment

Hudson Bay Capital Management senior strategist, February 2024 to approximately March 2025. Published the 41-page "A User's Guide to Restructuring the Global Trading System" for Hudson Bay in November 2024, the foundational document for the Mar-a-Lago Accord framework. Treasury senior advisor for economic strategy in Trump 1.0, April 2020 to January 2021 (influenced CARES Act design). Manhattan Institute senior fellow. PhD Economics, Harvard 2010 (advisors included Martin Feldstein); BA Boston University.

### OGE-278 financial disclosure

The Miran OGE-278 is available at extapps2.oge.gov (referenced in search results as "G Miran, Stephen I - Page 1"). The agency ethics official concluded Miran was in compliance with applicable laws. Senator Warren's February 21, 2025 letter (warren_miran_letter PDF) flagged Hudson Bay Capital portfolio companies as a conflict source; Miran's responses to Banking Committee Questions for the Record (September 7, 2025) addressed these. Specific Hudson Bay portfolio holdings and any direct crypto positions were not enumerated in the search pass reviewed.

### Position on EO 14178 and tariff-stablecoin framework

Miran's "User's Guide" is the foundational document for the Trump 2.0 trade-and-currency strategy. The relevant link to EO 14178: the user's-guide framework treats a dollar-backed stablecoin ecosystem as a tool of dollar reserve-currency dominance, which is also the explicit framing of the EO and Bessent's public statements. Miran himself disclaims policy-advocacy intent ("not policy advocacy, just an exploration") per Washington Post quotation in Fortune (2025).

### Top conflicts identified

(1) Hudson Bay is multi-strategy; portfolio holdings intersecting macro or crypto positions remain undisclosed in public summaries. (2) The unpaid-leave-of-absence mechanism does not formally sever the Hudson Bay employment relationship. (3) "User's Guide" was published while at Hudson Bay; the firm's positioning around the policies described is not in the public record.

## 5. William Pulte (FHFA Director)

### Government role

Confirmed March 13, 2025 by a 56–43 Senate vote (Senate Banking Committee 15–9 on March 6, 2025) as FHFA Director (Wikipedia, Bill Pulte; mortgagepoint.com). FHFA regulates Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac, the two government-sponsored enterprises that backstop roughly half of the $13 trillion U.S. residential mortgage market.

### Prior employment

Founder of Pulte Capital Partners (Bloomfield Hills, MI), 2011, a private investment firm. PulteGroup board director September 2016 to 2020. Following grandfather William J. Pulte's 2018 death, Bill Pulte was the only descendant to receive an inheritance per a subsequent lawsuit (Wikipedia). BA broadcast journalism, Northwestern.

### OGE-278 financial disclosure

Per ProPublica and reporting at crypto.news and The American Prospect: Bitcoin $500K to $1M; Solana $500K to $1M; MARA Holdings (the largest U.S. publicly-traded Bitcoin miner) equity position (range not specified in sources reviewed). Pulte did not divest these positions on taking the FHFA Directorship. Senate Democrats led by Sen. Merkley wrote to Pulte on the conflict between his "ability to order and approve" Fannie and Freddie proposals and his role chairing the Enterprises' boards, asking explicitly about divestment or recusal (Merkley letter, July 2025; Durbin letter).

### Position on EO 14178 and the crypto-mortgage directive

On June 25, 2025, Pulte issued a formal FHFA directive ordering Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac to "prepare a proposal for consideration of cryptocurrency as an asset for reserves in their respective single-family mortgage loan risk assessment, without conversion of said cryptocurrency to U.S. dollars" (Duane Morris Fintechnically Speaking; Alston Consumer Finance; CoinDesk June 25, 2025; American Prospect August 28, 2025). Public statement on X: "We will study the usage of cryptocurrency holdings as it relates to qualifying for mortgages."

### Top conflicts identified

(1) FHFA Director personally holding $500K to $1M each in BTC and SOL while issuing a directive that would expand the eligible-reserve definition to include those exact assets is the cleanest direct conflict in the cabinet roster. (2) MARA equity: the directive directly benefits the firms mining the underlying assets. (3) Family-trust PulteGroup inheritance creates a homebuilder-housing-finance-policy linkage adjacent to but distinct from the crypto conflict.

## 8. Pamela Bondi (Attorney General)

### Government role

Confirmed February 4, 2025 by a 54–46 Senate vote; sworn in February 5, 2025 as the 87th U.S. Attorney General. Per Wikipedia summary, fired April 2, 2026. (The post-firing AG line of succession is outside this dossier's scope.)

### Prior employment

Ballard Partners registered lobbyist and registered foreign agent, 2019 to January 2025. Florida Attorney General January 4, 2011 to January 8, 2019 (two four-year terms). Assistant State Attorney, Hillsborough County, 1994 to 2009. BA Florida (1987); JD Stetson (1990).

### Disclosed Ballard Partners clients

Per Senate Judiciary minority release and Wikipedia: State of Qatar (registered FARA work tied to 2022 FIFA World Cup anti-human-trafficking efforts); KGL KSCC (Kuwaiti logistics); GEO Group (for-profit prisons); Amazon; Uber; General Motors; Florida Sheriffs Association; more than 30 clients total. No crypto-industry clients surfaced in the search pass, though Ballard reported crypto-firm clients in the overall book per Sludge.

### OGE-278 financial disclosure

Filed January 2025. Specific asset list not enumerated in the search pass. Senate Judiciary minority release noted Bondi "did not list her current role as a lobbyist as a potential conflict of interest, nor any of the specific work she's done for her clients that might implicate her role as the chief federal law enforcement officer."

### Position on crypto enforcement

The Trump DOJ posture under Bondi shifted markedly: Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche issued an April 7, 2025 memo titled "Ending Regulation by Prosecution" directing DOJ to stop prosecuting mixers, exchanges, and offline wallets for end-user conduct. However, the memo's footnote preserved cases where business owners knowingly handle dirty money, and excluded the USC 18 § 1960 subsection underlying the Storm (Tornado Cash) and Samourai Wallet prosecutions, both of which continued (DL News; The Rage; Money Laundering News, December 2025; The Coin Republic, April 8, 2026). Trump separately directed Bondi to examine the Samourai Wallet case for potential developer pardons.

### Top conflicts identified

(1) Ballard client list overlaps DOJ enforcement jurisdiction (GEO Group on private-prison contracts; Qatar on FARA enforcement; Uber on antitrust). (2) The Blanche memo's selective scope is the most consequential crypto-enforcement-posture shift under Bondi's tenure and directly benefits the industry.

Private practice and counsel to Donald Trump in Trump v. United States (2024). Missouri Solicitor General January 2017 to 2023, appointed by then-Missouri AG Josh Hawley. Clerked for Justice Antonin Scalia and Judge J. Michael Luttig. JD Harvard; Rhodes Scholar.

### OGE-278 financial disclosure

### Crypto-related litigation positions

- IRS v. Coinbase user (James Harper case, Supreme Court docket 24-922): Sauer as Counsel of Record for the federal parties argued on May 30, 2025 that Harper has no Fourth Amendment right to shield his financial records held by Coinbase under the third-party doctrine. The government's position is that Harper "voluntarily" shared data with Coinbase and the IRS used proper procedures (judicially-approved "John Doe" summons) to obtain it (Cointelegraph; Coinpedia; Trading View summary of the Cointelegraph filing).
- SEC v. Coinbase (3d Cir. 23-3202): the Skadden inside-the-courts summary noted the appellate decision of January 13, 2025; subsequent DOJ amicus posture under Sauer was not located in the search pass.
- The SEC's January 21, 2025 dismissal of the Coinbase enforcement action (SEC press release 2025-47) preceded Sauer's confirmation; the dismissal was an SEC-internal decision through the new Crypto Task Force, not a Sauer brief.

### Position on EO 14178

Sauer's office is the Executive Branch's Supreme Court advocate. EO 14178 implementation depends on Solicitor-General defense of any challenged rulemaking. No direct Sauer statement on EO 14178 was located.

### Top conflicts identified

(1) Prior personal representation of Donald Trump in Trump v. United States is the dominant conflict, not crypto-specific; recusal scope from Trump-related litigation not fully documented. (2) Coinbase user case position is consistent with traditional third-party doctrine, not a conflict per se; flagged for completeness.

### Government role

NEC coordinates White House economic-policy strategy across Treasury, CEA, USTR, and OMB; Hassett chairs the White House Digital Assets Working Group through this position (per BiographyBrief and CryptoSlate summaries citing the working-group structure). Per Crypto Times on the June 2025 OGE filing: Coinbase Global (COIN) common stock $1M to $5M; total disclosed assets minimum $7.6M. Hassett did not divest the Coinbase position on taking the NEC role; the disclosure was filed roughly six months after he began the position.

### Position on EO 14178

Hassett chairs the White House Digital Assets Working Group structurally; the Working Group's July 2025 report on stablecoin and banking-related recommendations is the operational EO 14178 implementation document (whitehouse.gov fact sheet, July 2025; Arnold and Porter advisory). Hassett's public statements have endorsed: stablecoin regulatory framework (GENIUS Act), banks serving crypto businesses (rolling back SAB 121 successor guidance), and inclusion of Bitcoin in a national strategic reserve.

### Top conflicts identified

(1) $1M to $5M Coinbase position held during White House Digital Assets Working Group chairmanship is the structurally cleanest crypto conflict in the cabinet roster by dollar value; the Working Group's recommendations directly affect Coinbase's regulatory perimeter. (2) Coinbase Advisory Council pre-administration tenure documents a continuing relationship rather than a clean break. (3) Fed Chair candidacy creates a forward-looking conflict extending into monetary-policy authority.
