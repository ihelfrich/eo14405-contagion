# Tether / iFinex / Bitfinex / Cantor Fitzgerald nexus

*Dossier for EO 14405 contagion paper. Compiled May 2026.*

## Executive summary

Tether Holdings Limited (issuer of USDT, the world's largest stablecoin at roughly \$187 billion in circulating supply as of mid-2026 [CoinMarketCap]) sits at the center of three interlocking structural risks that EO 14405 amplifies rather than resolves. First, the issuer remains a BVI/Hong Kong entity whose ownership and reserve management have been the subject of two U.S. enforcement settlements (NY AG 2021, CFTC 2021) and a reported, unconfirmed DOJ criminal investigation out of the Manhattan U.S. Attorney's Office (WSJ Oct 25 2024). Second, Tether's principal U.S. counterparty for reserve custody is Cantor Fitzgerald, whose then-CEO Howard Lutnick is now U.S. Commerce Secretary and whose family acquired controlling interest in Cantor through a transaction reportedly financed in part by a Tether loan (Senate Warren/Wyden letters, Apr 30 2026). Third, Tether has launched a U.S.-domiciled GENIUS Act compliant sibling stablecoin, USAT, issued by Anchorage Digital Bank with Cantor as collateral custodian and former White House Crypto Council director Bo Hines as CEO (Anchorage / Tether press, Jan 27 2026). EO 14405's Limited Purpose Master Account pathway is the regulatory bridge that lets the offshore-USDT economic engine be re-papered into a U.S.-regulated wrapper without the parent ever submitting to U.S. consolidated supervision.

## Corporate structure (entity layer)

The "Tether" trade name resolves to a layered offshore structure under parent **iFinex Inc.** (BVI). Subsidiaries include:

- **Tether Holdings Limited** (BVI): the entity named in the CFTC 2021 order [CFTC PR 8450-21]
- **Tether Operations Limited**
- **Tether International Limited**
- **Tether Limited**: the entity named alongside iFinex in the NY AG settlement [NY AG Feb 23 2021]
- **Bitfinex**: the affiliated exchange operated through iFinex; "owned and controlled by the same small group of individuals" as Tether per the NY AG [NY AG Feb 23 2021]

Banking history runs: Wells Fargo correspondent access lost in 2017; subsequent dependence on Bahamian **Deltec Bank** which remains the disclosed banking partner; **Cantor Fitzgerald** now custodies the majority of the Treasury-bill leg of the reserve. Reserve attestation history runs Friedman LLP (resigned 2018) → Moore Cayman → **BDO Italia** (current) [Cryptopotato, BTC Times, Tether.io Q1 2026 attestation].

Devasini was promoted from CFO to Chairman of Tether in March 2025, further concentrating control [Wikipedia, Coinomist]. Stuart Hoegner, general counsel since 2014, retired in early 2025 and was succeeded by Michael Hilliard [Protos, The Block].

## Reserve composition history

### 2017–2018 controversy

Per the CFTC order [CFTC PR 8450-21, Oct 15 2021], from June 1 2016 through Feb 25 2019 Tether represented USDT as "100% backed by corresponding fiat currency." The CFTC found Tether held sufficient fiat reserves on **only 27.6% of the days in a 26-month sample**. Reserves included unsecured receivables and non-fiat assets; reserves were commingled with Bitfinex operational accounts; at least 29 funding arrangements lacked documentation. Bitfinex transferred over **\$382 million** into Tether's bank account before a scheduled reserve review. No professional audit was ever completed during the violation window.

### 2019 NY AG suit

NY AG Letitia James filed suit in 2019 after determining Bitfinex had lost roughly **\$850 million** through its Panama-based payment processor Crypto Capital Corp [NY AG Feb 23 2021]. The investigation also found Tether "had no access to banking, anywhere in the world" starting mid-2017, and that a 2018 Deltec verification was undermined when funds were transferred out of the verified account the following day, leaving USDT unbacked at the moment the public attestation was issued.

### 2021 CFTC + NY AG settlements

- **NY AG (Feb 23 2021):** \$18.5 million penalty; ban on serving New Yorkers; mandatory quarterly reporting on inter-entity transfers and reserve composition. iFinex and Tether Limited named as respondents. [NY AG press release]
- **CFTC (Oct 15 2021):** \$41 million penalty on Tether for misleading reserve representations; \$1.5 million penalty on Bitfinex for unregistered retail commodity transactions with U.S. customers (Mar 1 2016 to Dec 31 2018) in violation of a prior June 2 2016 CFTC order. [CFTC PR 8450-21]

### 2022–2024 disclosed composition

Tether shifted disclosed reserves heavily into U.S. Treasury bills over 2022–2024, with attestations conducted under ISAE 3000 (Revised) by BDO. These remain *attestations*, not audits. No Big Four audit has ever been completed.

### Current attestation regime (Q1 2026)

Per Tether's published BDO attestation [Tether.io, May 2026]: direct and indirect exposure to U.S. Treasury bills of approximately **\$141 billion** as of March 31 2026; roughly 80% of reserves in Treasuries; approximately **\$20 billion in physical gold** and **\$7 billion in BTC**; an additional excess-reserve buffer of **\$8.23 billion**; Q1 2026 net profit of \$1.04 billion. BDO concluded the reserve report was "fairly presented" under the stated criteria, not an audit opinion. [BDO via Tether.io; Cryptopotato; BTC Times]

USDT supply trajectory: 1M (Jan 2016) → 100M (May 2017) → 1B (Dec 2017) → 10B (Jun 2020) → 50B (Apr 2021) → ~187B (May 2026). [CoinMarketCap / CoinGecko historical]

## Cantor Fitzgerald connection

### Howard Lutnick's role
Lutnick was CEO of Cantor Fitzgerald, a primary dealer in U.S. Treasuries, when Cantor became the principal custodian of Tether's Treasury-bill reserve. He was nominated as Commerce Secretary on Nov 19 2024 and confirmed in February 2025 with **106 identified conflicts of interest** [CBS News].

### Cantor's USDT T-bill custody
Cantor holds the majority of Tether's \$130B+ Treasury position and is paid "tens of millions of dollars" annually in fees [Crowdfund Insider; Cointelegraph; The Block, all reporting on the WSJ disclosure, Nov 2024]. Cantor will also custody the collateral for the new USAT stablecoin [Anchorage press, Jan 27 2026].

### Cantor's 5% equity stake in Tether
First reported by the Wall Street Journal in November 2024 [republished via The Block, Crowdfund Insider, Cointelegraph]: Cantor Fitzgerald negotiated a 5% equity stake in Tether, structured as a roughly **\$600 million convertible bond**. Reporting in late 2025 valued this stake against a Tether private-round valuation approaching \$500 billion [Disruption Banking, Sep 25 2025].

### Conflict-of-interest implications
Lutnick formally divested from Cantor in October 2025 after assuming the Commerce role. **The divestiture vehicle is the live problem.** Reporting [Crain Currency; Bitcoin News; Cryptotimes; Odaily] indicates Lutnick's son Brandon acquired majority control of Cantor through Dynasty Trust A, and that the transaction was financed in part by a **loan from Tether**, the same firm whose reserves Cantor custodies and in which Cantor holds a 5% equity stake. Senators Warren (D-MA) and Wyden (D-OR) opened a fourth round of inquiry on **April 30 2026**, asking explicitly whether "Tether may have helped provide your children with the capital needed to purchase your stake in Cantor Fitzgerald, and in return, secured an interest in your children's assets" [DL News; Cryptotimes; Bitcoin News, Apr 30 2026]. Responses were demanded by May 13 2026. Public status as of this writing: no substantive disclosure from either party; no enforcement action.

The structural concern is straightforward. A foreign stablecoin issuer with two U.S. settlements on its record and a reported open DOJ criminal investigation is the lender to the family trust of a sitting Cabinet secretary whose department has direct equities in (a) the master-account question through coordination with Treasury and the FSOC, (b) export-control regimes that touch crypto sanctions, and (c) the broader USD-as-reserve-currency posture that the GENIUS Act and EO 14405 advance.

## US regulatory and enforcement posture

### 2021 settlements
See above. Combined \$61M, no admission of fraud, no individual liability, no operating ban outside NY.

### Ongoing DOJ investigation (status, best knowable)
The Wall Street Journal reported on **October 25 2024** that the U.S. Attorney's Office for the Southern District of New York (Manhattan) is conducting a criminal investigation of Tether for possible sanctions and AML violations, focused on use of USDT by third parties for drug trafficking, terrorism finance, and hacking proceeds. The Treasury Department was separately reported to be considering OFAC sanctions on Tether itself given USDT's circulation among SDNs including Hamas-linked entities and Russian arms dealers. [Fortune; The Daily Hodl; CNBC] Ardoino denied any knowledge of an investigation on X the same day. **As of May 2026 there has been no public charge, no public settlement, and no formal confirmation from DOJ or Treasury.** The status is "reported, denied, unresolved."

### Sanctions enforcement history (OFAC USDT-related)
Tether's posture diverges from Circle's. Circle (USDC) froze all USDC at Tornado Cash addresses immediately upon the Aug 8 2022 OFAC designation. Tether took the position that stablecoin issuers were not clearly obligated to freeze sanctioned addresses [Chainalysis]. Tether's cooperative-freeze posture has since shifted: it has publicly announced cumulative wallet freezes totaling at least **\$344 million** in coordination with OFAC and U.S. law enforcement [Tether.io], including approximately **\$27M tied to sanctioned Russian exchange Garantex** and a separate **\$23M** Secret Service freeze related to Garantex transfers [Cointelegraph; Tether.io]. The pattern: cooperative when asked, asset-controlling when convenient, not proactively compliant with OFAC SDN lists in the way Circle has been.

### Likely regulatory pathway under EO 14405
USDT itself is structurally ineligible for a U.S. Limited Purpose Master Account: the issuer is BVI, has unresolved criminal investigation exposure, and has never submitted to U.S. consolidated supervision. The vehicle is **USAT**, the GENIUS Act sibling stablecoin issued by **Anchorage Digital Bank, N.A.** (OCC-chartered) and launched **January 27 2026** [Anchorage Digital press; PYMNTS; Yahoo Finance]. Anchorage already holds an OCC national trust bank charter, the cleanest existing federal stablecoin issuance license. Collateral custody sits with Cantor. CEO is **Bo Hines**, former Executive Director of the White House Crypto Council. The U.S. government will treat USDT as a "foreign stablecoin issuer" and USAT as the domestic compliance window.

## EO 14405 implications for Tether specifically

The structural question is whether the economic activity of USDT can be migrated to USAT without losing the offshore optionality that made USDT dominant in the first place.

1. **Most-likely master-account-bearing entity:** Anchorage Digital Bank (already OCC-chartered). The LPMA pathway is a refinement, not a prerequisite, for Anchorage. USAT inherits this. Cantor functions as Treasury-bill custodian and possibly as the broker into FedNow settlement on the wholesale side.

2. **FedNow integration path:** Anchorage as an OCC-chartered national trust bank does not automatically have a Reserve Bank master account; the LPMA framework under EO 14405 is precisely the vehicle that gives stablecoin-issuing OCC banks direct Fed access for reserve management and FedNow rails. USAT is plausibly the first or second domestic stablecoin to acquire LPMA + FedNow access if the rulemaking proceeds on the timeline implied by the EO.

3. **The offshore-onshore arbitrage:** USDT continues to serve non-U.S. users (the bulk of demand) without GENIUS Act constraints; USAT serves U.S. institutional users with full federal compliance. Tether's parent retains the offshore profit engine while gaining a U.S. policy seat through USAT. This is functionally the same structure tobacco and pharma companies used in the 1990s, separate domestic-compliance subsidiary, parent retains the unregulated upstream.

4. **The conflict-of-interest channel:** Cantor's dual role (Tether equity holder, USDT reserve custodian, USAT collateral custodian, Commerce Secretary's family trust beneficiary, Tether borrower) creates a unique configuration where master-account decisions by the Fed are made against a backdrop of senior executive-branch financial entanglement with the applicant. The Warren/Wyden inquiry is the most concrete legislative-branch pushback so far; no judicial or executive-branch action has materialized.
