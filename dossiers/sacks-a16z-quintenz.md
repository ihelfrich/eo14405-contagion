# White House AI/Crypto Czar + a16z + Quintenz nexus

## Executive summary

David Sacks, a Craft Ventures co-founder and All-In Podcast co-host, served as White House AI and Crypto Czar from January 2025 to March 2026 as a Special Government Employee (SGE), the 130-day category that requires neither Senate confirmation nor full public disclosure. Before assuming the role, Sacks and Craft Ventures reportedly divested over \$200 million in crypto assets, including Bitcoin, Ether, Solana, the Bitwise 10 Crypto Index Fund, and Coinbase and Robinhood equity, though Office of Government Ethics filings were never made public and a March 5, 2025 ethics waiver from White House Counsel David Warrington covered "matters of general applicability concerning the digital asset industry" (whitehouse.gov, Sacks memo, 2025-03-05). Andreessen Horowitz committed at least \$47 million to the Fairshake super PAC across the 2024 and 2026 cycles and placed its global head of crypto policy, Brian Quintenz, in the CFTC chair nomination chair (later withdrawn). The pattern is a textbook revolving door: a regulated industry funding super PAC infrastructure while seeing its policy staff and portfolio executives placed in or adjacent to the agencies that write its rules.

## David Sacks

### Government role

Trump announced Sacks as "White House A.I. & Crypto Czar" on December 5, 2024 (Trump statement archived at presidency.ucsb.edu). The stated scope: AI and crypto policy guidance, a legal framework for the crypto industry, oversight of the President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology (PCAST). Sacks served as a Special Government Employee (SGE), capped at 130 working days per year. He stepped down from the czar role in March 2026 and transferred to a presidential advisory committee role (CNBC, 2026-03-26; CoinDesk, 2026-03-26).

### Private portfolio (Craft Ventures + personal)

Craft Ventures' publicly listed portfolio includes Bitwise (the crypto index fund manager, listed at craftventures.com/portfolio/bitwise). Prior to taking the czar role, Sacks personally and via Craft Ventures held positions in Bitcoin, Ether, Solana, the Bitwise 10 Crypto Index Fund, and equity in Coinbase and Robinhood, as well as limited-partner interests in Multicoin Capital and Blockchain Capital (CNBC, 2025-03-14; Fortune, 2025-03-14). Per the White House memo, these were divested before the role began. Remaining disclosed holdings include equity in BitGo (crypto custody, roughly 2.5% of Sacks's assets per ethics memo reporting) and Lightning Labs (Bitcoin protocol development, roughly 1.1%) (Daily Hodl, 2025-03-16; Cointelegraph, 2025-03).

### Public statements on EO 14405 and stablecoin policy

Sacks publicly championed the January 23, 2025 executive order "Strengthening American Leadership in Digital Financial Technology" and the subsequent GENIUS Act (stablecoin framework) and CLARITY Act (market structure) as they moved through Congress, discussed on multiple All-In Podcast episodes through 2025 and 2026. The EO bans a US central bank digital currency and explicitly endorses dollar-backed stablecoins as a tool of dollar sovereignty (whitehouse.gov, 2025-01-23; Holland & Knight, 2025-01; Reed Smith, 2025).

### Ethics filings and recusals

White House Counsel David Warrington issued Sacks a written ethics waiver on March 5, 2025, releasing him from conflict-of-interest restrictions "regarding particular matters of general applicability concerning the digital asset industry" (whitehouse.gov memo, posted at /wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Memo-David-Sacks-3.5.2025-1.pdf). Senator Elizabeth Warren and Rep. Melanie Stansbury opened a congressional investigation into Sacks's ethics arrangement in 2025 (warren.senate.gov, 2025; Banking Committee minority letter to OGE). Sacks's full OGE-278 financial disclosure has not been released publicly; the public has no independent way to verify divestment claims (New York Times reporting summarized at chaincatcher.com, 2025).

### Political donations

Sacks hosted a June 2024 San Francisco fundraiser that raised approximately \$12 million for the Trump campaign (Wikipedia, "David Sacks", citing Bloomberg and Reuters). Direct Stand With Crypto / Fairshake personal-name contributions by Sacks are not confirmed in OpenSecrets summaries reviewed for this dossier; Craft Ventures as an entity is not a primary Fairshake donor in published Fairshake top-donor lists, which are dominated by Coinbase, a16z, Ripple, and Jump Crypto. (Flagged in Uncertainty.)

## a16z Crypto Fund

### Fund sizes and portfolio

a16z crypto has raised five dedicated crypto funds. Crypto Fund IV closed at \$4.5 billion in 2022 (TechCrunch, 2026-05-05; a16zcrypto.com). Crypto Fund V closed at \$2.2 billion in May 2026 (TechCrunch, 2026-05-05; KuCoin blog). Chris Dixon, who founded a16z crypto in 2018, has described over \$7 billion in committed capital across the platform (a16zcrypto.com/team/chris-dixon). The broader a16z firm raised approximately \$15 billion in 2024 across AI, crypto, and "American Dynamism" funds (IndexBox; tradersunion.com).

Portfolio companies relevant to this dossier (per a16zcrypto.com posts and reporting): Coinbase (pre-IPO Series B and later), Solana (one of the earliest backers), Uniswap, OpenSea, Phantom, Kalshi, EigenLayer (additional \$70 million direct token deal in 2025 for EigenCloud, on top of prior \$100 million Series B; Decrypt 2025; The Block 2025), LayerZero (\$55 million 2025 deal), Optimism, Helium, Yuga Labs, and Jito (\$50 million direct token purchase from Solana ecosystem; The Block).

### Key personnel and roles

- Marc Andreessen, co-founder and general partner
- Ben Horowitz, co-founder and general partner
- Chris Dixon, founder of a16z crypto, general partner, author of "Read Write Own" (Random House, January 2024)
- Brian Quintenz, Global Head of Policy for a16z crypto from December 2022 to 2025
- Scott Kupor, former managing partner, departed a16z in 2025 to head the federal Office of Personnel Management for the Trump administration (Yahoo News / Fortune reporting)
- Anne Neuberger, former Biden White House senior cyber official, joined a16z as senior adviser in August 2025 (Axios, 2025-08-21)

### Political and policy spend

a16z committed approximately \$47 million across the 2024 and 2026 Fairshake super PAC cycles per public reporting:
- 2024 cycle: about \$24 million in cumulative commitments (one of three lead donors with Coinbase and Ripple; Coinbase about \$75 million was the largest single donor; CNBC 2024-11-04; CoinDesk 2024-11-04)
- 2026 cycle: an additional \$23 million contributed in November 2024 (CoinDesk, 2024-11-04)
- More recent figures cited by Citation Needed (2026) and CNBC (2025-01-30) put Coinbase at \$56 million, Ripple at \$48 million, and a16z at roughly \$24 million in 2026-cycle commitments, with Fairshake holding \$116 million on hand as of January 2025

### Specific positions on EO 14405

a16z crypto has endorsed the January 23, 2025 EO publicly via Dixon and a16z policy posts. Dixon's X post on stablecoins (status/1910098121448964424) frames stablecoins as "payments without intermediaries," echoing the EO's dollar-stablecoin-sovereignty framing.

## Marc Andreessen

### Personal donations and political role

Andreessen and Horowitz each personally donated \$2.5 million to Trump's super PAC in 2024 (Wikipedia, Marc Andreessen page, citing FEC filings). Andreessen said in late 2024 he was spending roughly half his time at Mar-a-Lago (multiple outlets summarized at calcalistech.com). He was named to PCAST in 2025 alongside Coinbase co-founder Fred Ehrsam (FinTech Weekly, 2026).

### Public statements

The October 16, 2023 "Techno-Optimist Manifesto" (a16z.com/the-techno-optimist-manifesto) is a 5,200-word essay arguing for unconstrained technological growth and against regulation, antitrust, and "deceleration." Andreessen has publicly attributed his shift toward Trump to the Biden administration's proposed tax on unrealized capital gains and its crypto enforcement posture (Breitbart 2024-07-18; TechCrunch 2024-07-16).

## Ben Horowitz

Horowitz matched Andreessen's \$2.5 million Trump super PAC contribution in 2024 and co-signed the July 2024 TechCrunch piece announcing the firm's institutional shift toward Trump (TechCrunch, 2024-07-16). Horowitz has been less publicly visible than Andreessen in PCAST and Mar-a-Lago meetings but co-signs a16z firm-level policy positions through internal and a16zcrypto.com posts.

## Brian Quintenz

### CFTC tenure (2017 to 2021)

Quintenz was confirmed to the CFTC on August 3, 2017 and seated August 15, 2017. His statutory term expired in April 2020; he stayed on under holdover authority and resigned effective August 31, 2021 (CFTC press release, 2021-08-19, "Statement of Commissioner Brian D. Quintenz on the End of His Term"; Wikipedia, Brian Quintenz). He chaired the agency's Technology Advisory Committee, which under his leadership ran the most extensive set of crypto and DeFi public hearings of any federal financial regulator at that time (Fenwick "Meet CFTC Nominee Brian Quintenz", 2025).

### a16z role (2021 to 2025)

Quintenz joined Andreessen Horowitz's crypto venture team in December 2022, was named Global Head of Crypto Policy at a16z crypto, and held that position through 2025 (Cointelegraph; DL News).

### CFTC Chair nomination timeline

- February 2025: Trump indicated intent to nominate Quintenz as CFTC Chair
- May 28, 2025: Quintenz disclosed he would resign from Kalshi's board if confirmed (Yogonet, 2025-05-28)
- June 10, 2025: Senate Agriculture Committee hearing scheduled (The Block, 2025)
- Financial disclosure: \$3.4 million in crypto-linked assets including stakes in three a16z funds (CNK Fund III, CNK Seed 1 Fund, CNK IV Fund), Kalshi stock and unvested options, and Next Level Derivatives (Crypto.news; Coinpedia; cryptonews.com, 2025). Ethics pledges: divest within 90 days, recuse from a16z matters for two years, from Kalshi matters for one year
- September 2025: After Tyler and Cameron Winklevoss publicly pressured the nomination over Gemini's CFTC inspector general complaint, the White House withdrew Quintenz (The Hill, 2025; NOTUS, 2025)
- December 2025: Michael Selig sworn in as 16th CFTC Chair instead
- January 6, 2026: Quintenz joined SUI Group's board as an independent director (Business Wire / Nasdaq, 2026-01-06; CoinDesk, 2026-01-06)

### Crypto regulatory positions

Quintenz argued throughout his commissioner tenure and a16z tenure for narrow, "risk-focused" CFTC jurisdiction over digital commodities (Bitcoin and Ether spot markets), against SEC jurisdictional expansion via the Howey test, and for a clear classification regime (eventually embodied in the CLARITY Act framework). His written Senate testimony pledged "clear crypto classification" (The Block).

## Chris Dixon

Dixon founded a16z crypto in 2018. His book "Read Write Own: Building the Next Era of the Internet" (Random House, January 2024) argues that blockchain-based ownership is the third era of the internet after read-only (Web 1) and read-write (Web 2). Dixon is an active OpenSecrets-documented donor to crypto-friendly members of Congress across both parties (Fortune, 2024-01-25). No formal congressional testimony by Dixon was located in this dossier's search pass (flagged in Uncertainty).

## Uncertainty

1. **EO 14405 number unconfirmed.** Sources consistently describe the January 23, 2025 "Strengthening American Leadership in Digital Financial Technology" executive order as the central crypto EO, but no source in this search pass labels it "EO 14405" by number. The Federal Register catalogues Trump 2025 EOs ranging through at least 14371; 14405 may be a later EO in the same sequence (possibly the Strategic Bitcoin Reserve EO of March 2025 or a derivative). Recommend pulling the Federal Register PDF directly to lock the number-to-title mapping before publication.
2. **Sacks's full OGE-278 financial disclosure is not public.** Divestment claims rest on a self-reported memo and the White House Counsel's waiver. The remaining BitGo and Lightning Labs equity percentages are reported figures, not first-source numbers.
3. **Direct Sacks Fairshake / Stand With Crypto donations** under his personal name not confirmed in public OpenSecrets summaries reviewed. The \$12 million figure refers to the June 2024 SF fundraiser he hosted, not his personal contribution.
4. **Chris Dixon congressional testimony** not located in this search; possible it occurred but was not surfaced. Recommend congress.gov direct search by witness name.
5. **a16z lobbying disclosures (LDA Form LD-2) and White House visitor logs** were not pulled in this pass. Visitor logs in particular would tighten the chronology of who met whom and when at 1600 Pennsylvania.
6. **Exact Fairshake cumulative totals** vary across reporting outlets, partly because Fairshake aggregates multiple affiliated PACs (Defend American Jobs, Protect Progress). The \$47M cumulative a16z figure is an approximate sum of public commitments and should be verified against FEC Form 3X filings before paper publication.
