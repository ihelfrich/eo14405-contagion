## Executive summary

Executive Order 14405, which extended Federal Reserve master-account access pathways to a defined class of qualified stablecoin issuers and tightened the Treasury bill collateral channel for non-bank dollar-tokens, sits on top of a small and tightly interlocked governance network. Five clusters of people effectively run the operating layer that the order privileges: the Circle officer and board roster, the Coinbase officer and board roster, BlackRock's executive committee and board, the BNY Mellon and State Street custodian benches, and a Cantor Fitzgerald-Tether axis that runs through Howard and Brandon Lutnick.

The interlocks that matter are not the famous ones. The famous interlocks (Marc Andreessen on Coinbase, Larry Fink chairing BlackRock) are visible in the proxy. The load-bearing interlocks are quieter: M. Michele Burns sits on Circle's audit committee while also sitting on the Goldman Sachs Group board, which puts a Goldman director one step from the issuer of the dollar-token whose reserves Goldman's former Chief Risk Officer (Craig Broderick, also on Circle's board) oversees from the risk-committee chair. Brian Brooks, who ran the OCC during the Anchorage Digital trust-charter approval, now sits on the BitGo board after a tour through Coinbase as Chief Legal Officer and Binance.US as CEO. Heath Tarbert went from CFTC Chair to Citadel Securities Chief Legal Officer to Circle's President in under four years. Dante Disparte ran policy for Libra/Diem and now runs policy for Circle; Sunita Parasuraman ran Treasury for Libra/Diem and now sits on the BitGo board. Katie Biber was General Counsel of Anchorage, then Chief Legal Officer at Brex, then Paradigm; she remains on Anchorage's board. The same forty or fifty people show up at every node.

BlackRock's specific position deserves its own section because it is unusual in two ways. First, BlackRock manages roughly ninety percent of USDC's non-bank reserve assets through the Circle Reserve Fund under a March 2025 Memorandum of Understanding, which means a single asset manager controls the marketable-securities side of the second-largest dollar stablecoin. Second, BlackRock's tokenized money-market fund BUIDL is issued through Securitize, which is itself raising capital via a SPAC and is the on-chain plumbing for the next generation of tokenized Treasuries. The composition of BlackRock's board (Larry Fink, Robert Kapito, Susan Wagner inside; William Ford of General Atlantic, Gordon Nixon of Royal Bank of Canada, Marco Antonio Slim Domit of Grupo Financiero Inbursa, Amin Nasser of Saudi Aramco outside; Gregg Lemkau of BDT and MSD added in 2026) tells you that the firm sees itself less as an asset manager than as the institutional layer where post-EO 14405 stablecoin reserves, tokenized Treasuries, and ETF baskets all converge.

The political contribution layer is concentrated on Coinbase and is the largest single source of corporate political money in the 2024 cycle. Coinbase contributed over seventy-five million dollars to the Fairshake super-PAC and its affiliates; Brian Armstrong personally added another million; Circle contributed one million USDC to the Trump inaugural committee. Public Citizen filed an FEC complaint alleging that Coinbase's contributions were unlawful because Coinbase is a federal contractor; the complaint has not been adjudicated.

The interlock graph is small, the regulator-to-private door spins quickly, and the political money flows in one direction. That triangulation is the load-bearing structural fact of post-EO 14405 stablecoin governance.

## BlackRock connection specifically

BlackRock occupies a structurally unique position post-EO 14405. Three lines of business converge.

First, the Circle Reserve Fund. Under a March 2025 Memorandum of Understanding (reported in trade press and confirmed in Circle's S-1 and DEF 14A risk-factor disclosures), BlackRock manages approximately ninety percent of USDC's non-bank reserves. The Circle Reserve Fund is a 2a-7 government money-market fund custodied with BNY Mellon; BlackRock's fixed-income team selects and manages the underlying Treasury bill basket. This makes BlackRock the largest single non-bank holder of short-duration Treasury exposure attributable to a stablecoin issuer, a position roughly comparable in size to a mid-size sovereign reserve manager.

Second, BUIDL. The BlackRock USD Institutional Digital Liquidity Fund launched March 2024 on the Ethereum network and is tokenized by Securitize. BUIDL functions as an on-chain money-market fund redeemable in USDC; the redemption pathway runs Securitize-to-Circle-to-Coinbase or directly to USDC. BUIDL has been extended to BNB Chain and is accepted as collateral on Binance. BlackRock filed a second tokenized fund with the SEC in 2025, again using Securitize as on-chain infrastructure.

Third, IBIT and ETHA. The iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT) and iShares Ethereum Trust (ETHA) are the two largest spot-crypto ETFs in the world. IBIT controls approximately 46.5 billion USD of the roughly 100 billion USD in Bitcoin ETF assets as of early 2026. Robert Mitchnick, Managing Director and Head of Digital Assets at BlackRock, runs the IBIT, ETHA, and BUIDL product lines.

The people who operationally connect BlackRock to the stablecoin ecosystem:
