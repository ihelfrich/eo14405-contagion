# Senate Banking + House Financial Services: conflict-of-interest dossier

*Companion to the EO 14405 contagion working paper. Compiled May 2026 from FEC committee filings, OpenSecrets PAC profiles, Senate and House financial disclosures (eFD / Clerk.house.gov), committee press releases, Congress.gov bill text, and primary news reporting. Cross-reference with `fairshake-campaign-finance.md` for PAC architecture and aggregate cycle figures.*

## Executive summary

The 119th Congress (sworn in 3 January 2025) inherited a Senate Banking Committee and a House Financial Services Committee whose median position on digital-asset policy moved sharply toward the industry between the close of the 118th Congress and the opening of the 119th. The displacement is traceable in three observable layers: (1) the Fairshake-Defend American Jobs-Protect Progress PAC complex spent roughly $196 million in independent expenditures across the 2024 cycle, with a 53-5 win-loss record, and the largest single-target buy ($40.1 million in Defend American Jobs spending against then-Banking-Chair Sherrod Brown in Ohio) eliminated the principal Senate-side blocker of industry-favored legislation (OpenSecrets PAC profile; CoinDesk, 26 September 2024; CNBC, 5 November 2024); (2) the new Republican majority installed Senator Tim Scott (R-SC) as Banking Chair and Senator Cynthia Lummis (R-WY) as Chair of a newly created Subcommittee on Digital Assets, with Lummis publicly disclosing personal bitcoin holdings reported as exceeding $100,000 (Cointelegraph; Senate eFD filings); (3) the House installed Representative French Hill (R-AR), a career banker who founded Delta Trust & Banking in Little Rock and served as a senior executive at Regions Bank, as chair of Financial Services, and named Representative Bryan Steil (R-WI), a Fairshake-supported incumbent, to chair the Digital Assets, Financial Technology, and Artificial Intelligence Subcommittee (Encyclopedia of Arkansas; OpenSecrets independent-expenditure data; committee press releases).

The legislative output of these committees in 2025 was, in order, the GENIUS Act (S. 394, payment stablecoin framework; signed 18 July 2025), the CLARITY Act / Digital Asset Market Clarity Act (introduced 29 May 2025; House-passed 294-132 in July 2025), the BITCOIN Act of 2025 (S. 954, Strategic Bitcoin Reserve; Lummis-Begich), and a market structure bill that cleared Senate Banking 18-6 in May 2026 (Roll Call, 15 May 2026). Executive Order 14405, signed 6 March 2025, established the Strategic Bitcoin Reserve and the Digital Asset Stockpile in advance of any of these legislative artifacts, but the political conditions for the order were set during the 2024 cycle in which the PAC complex described above operated. The proximate question this dossier addresses is which members of the two committees have financial or familial entanglements with the firms whose interests EO 14405 and its statutory follow-ons materially advance.

The highest-risk conflicts, on the criteria assembled here, cluster in five members: Cynthia Lummis (personal bitcoin holdings while chairing the Senate digital-assets subcommittee and sponsoring the BITCOIN Act); French Hill (career banker chairing the committee whose stablecoin master-account scope is at issue); Bryan Steil (direct $764,206 in Fairshake independent-expenditure support while chairing the House digital-assets subcommittee); Andy Barr (more than $7 million in Fairshake ads in his successful 2026 Senate primary, plus $2.3 million to an aligned dark-money PAC from Crypto.com parent Foris DAX, while still sitting on the House Financial Services Committee); and Tim Scott (Banking Chair, GENIUS Act co-sponsor, recipient of crypto-executive contributions reported at ~$64,700 across the 2024 cycle).

## House Financial Services Committee

### French Hill (Chair, R-AR)

- **Fairshake receipts:** Re-elected 2024 in AR-02. Specific dollar figure for Fairshake or Defend American Jobs IE spending in Hill's race not isolated in public reporting reviewed for this dossier [unverified, race-level FEC IE filings not exhaustively reviewed].
- **Disclosed crypto holdings:** None located in publicly reviewed coverage [unverified, original House financial disclosure PDF not retrieved for this compilation].
- **Sponsored crypto bills:** Hill is the primary House sponsor on multiple crypto vehicles. Original co-sponsor and lead House Republican on **CLARITY Act / Digital Asset Market Clarity Act** (introduced 29 May 2025; House-passed 294-132 in July 2025). Co-sponsor of the **STABLE Act** (House companion / variant of the GENIUS Act framework). Chaired the Digital Assets, Financial Technology, and Inclusion Subcommittee in the 118th Congress before ascending to the full-committee chair in the 119th.
- **Family financial-services ties:** Hill himself is the relevant financial-services tie: he is a third-generation banker, founder, chairman, and CEO of **Delta Trust & Banking Corp.** in Little Rock (1999 to its 2014 acquisition by Simmons First National), and prior to that a senior executive at **Regions Bank** in Little Rock and at First Commercial Corporation. He served as a senior Treasury official under President George H. W. Bush in the early 1990s (Encyclopedia of Arkansas; Arkansas Business). ## Top 10 highest-risk conflicts in the 119th Congress on stablecoin policy

Ranked by combination of (a) direct personal financial exposure to the regulated industry, (b) magnitude of campaign-finance support received from the industry's PAC complex, and (c) institutional position in the committee or subcommittee with primary jurisdiction.

1. **Cynthia Lummis (R-WY)**: Personal bitcoin holdings reported in excess of $100,000 while serving as Chair of the Senate Banking Subcommittee on Digital Assets and lead sponsor of the BITCOIN Act of 2025, which directs the federal government to acquire 1 million bitcoin over five years. This is the clearest direct-financial-exposure-plus-jurisdiction overlap in either chamber.
2. **French Hill (R-AR)**: Career commercial banker (Delta Trust founder, Regions Bank senior executive) now chairing the committee whose remit includes the bank-versus-crypto cleavage at the heart of stablecoin master-account scope. The conflict here is sectoral rather than crypto-personal but is direct and longstanding.
3. **Bryan Steil (R-WI)**: $764,206 in Fairshake independent-expenditure support for 2024 re-election, now chairing the House Digital Assets Subcommittee and lead sponsor of the STABLE Act.
4. **Andy Barr (R-KY)**: More than $7 million in Fairshake support for 2026 Senate primary, plus $2.3 million to aligned Keep America Great PAC from Crypto.com parent Foris DAX, while still serving on House Financial Services. The largest live PAC-receipt-to-sitting-member exposure in either committee.
5. **Bernie Moreno (R-OH)**: $40.1 million in Defend American Jobs IE support for 2024 Senate election, now sitting on Senate Banking. Documented separately in `fairshake-campaign-finance.md`.
6. **Ruben Gallego (D-AZ)**: $10 million in Protect Progress IE support for 2024 Senate election, now top Democrat on the Senate Banking Subcommittee on Digital Assets. Note: the in-cycle position of the recipient varies (Gallego has, in negotiation with Lummis, pushed for stricter administration-official ethics rules), but the cycle-magnitude support places him on the list.
7. **Bill Hagerty (R-TN)**: Lead Senate sponsor of GENIUS Act; ~$98,000 in crypto-executive personal contributions reported across 2023-2024 cycle. Position-jurisdiction match without any documented personal asset exposure on the public record reviewed.
8. **Tim Scott (R-SC)**: Senate Banking Chair, GENIUS Act co-sponsor, ~$64,700 in crypto-executive personal contributions in 2024 cycle. Listed here for institutional-position reasons even though personal financial exposure is lower than several other ranked members.
9. **Pete Ricketts (R-NE)**: Family TD Ameritrade brokerage equity, while a legacy non-crypto exposure, becomes relevant in market-structure provisions that affect broker-dealer rules. Listed for sectoral conflict at the brokerage-versus-crypto-exchange boundary.
10. **Bill Huizenga (R-MI)**: Vice Chair of House Financial Services; co-sponsor of FIT21, CLARITY, and STABLE. No documented personal asset exposure; listed for institutional position and consistent voting record.

## Uncertainty

Several specific factual claims in this dossier are flagged as `[unverified]`. The pattern is consistent: aggregate Fairshake / Defend American Jobs / Protect Progress totals are well-sourced from OpenSecrets, FEC, and contemporaneous reporting, but **race-level independent-expenditure breakdowns are not reliably available for every named race**, particularly for House races and for incumbents in safe districts where IE spending was small or absent. Personal-capacity itemized FEC contributions from crypto executives were not re-extracted from raw FEC committee receipts for this compilation; I have relied on secondary reporting (notably the Washington Examiner / Denver Gazette piece of July 2025) and have flagged these as such.

Personal financial disclosures: I did not retrieve original Senate eFD PDFs or House Clerk disclosure PDFs for individual members. For the highest-risk personal-asset case (Lummis's bitcoin holdings), the original disclosure has been reported in multiple secondary outlets (Cointelegraph, Cointelegraph profile; news coverage of her early-2022 disclosure of a 2021 bitcoin purchase between $50,001 and $100,000, subsequently characterized as growing past $100,000 in later coverage) but the precise current dollar value is not pinned to a primary source in this compilation. The figure should be treated as ">~$100,000" rather than as a point estimate.

Family employment ties: I have searched for immediate-family employment at Coinbase, Ripple, a16z, Circle, Paxos, Anchorage, Cantor, Custodia, and major bank lobbyists, and have located no positive matches in publicly reviewed reporting. The verified-absence claims in the body should be read as "no public reporting located" rather than as exhaustive verification, particularly for spouses' employment at firms below S&P 500 / household-name visibility.

Speaker fees: I have not retrieved the relevant annual financial-disclosure schedule (typically Schedule D / Earned Income) for any individual member for the 2024 reporting year. Where I report "none located," I mean none reported in secondary coverage. A direct retrieval of the original disclosures from disclosures-clerk.house.gov and efdsearch.senate.gov would be a useful follow-up extension of this dossier.

Litigation: I did not run the `forensic-econ osint court` query against each named member individually within the time budget for this compilation. For the highest-risk five (Lummis, Hill, Steil, Barr, Scott), no relevant litigation has surfaced in standard reporting. For the others, a clean follow-up pass would close out the litigation column.

The structural finding (that the 119th Congress's committee composition, leadership, and legislative output are visibly downstream of the 2024-cycle Fairshake spend) does not depend on these flagged uncertainties. The aggregate facts (committee chair turnover, $40 million Ohio spend, 53-5 PAC record, GENIUS Act passage in July 2025) are robust to any plausible correction of the secondary figures above.
