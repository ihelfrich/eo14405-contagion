# Crypto campaign finance: Fairshake, Stand With Crypto, and the 2024 cycle

*Dossier for the EO 14405 contagion working paper. Compiled May 2026 from FEC, OpenSecrets, ProPublica, LDA, and primary news reporting.*

## Executive summary

In the 2023-2024 election cycle the cryptocurrency industry consolidated three previously dispersed political instruments into a single, well-capitalized electoral machine. The hybrid super PAC **Fairshake** and its two partisan affiliates, **Defend American Jobs** (Republican-facing) and **Protect Progress** (Democratic-facing), together raised roughly \$260 million and spent close to \$196 million on independent expenditures targeted at a relatively small number of federal races (FEC C00835959; OpenSecrets summary, 2024). Coinbase, Ripple Labs, and individuals affiliated with Andreessen Horowitz supplied roughly 84 percent of that money. The PAC complex ended the cycle reporting a win-loss record of 53 wins to 5 losses across the races where it intervened (CNBC, 26 June 2024; CoinDesk, 2 December 2024). Combined with Coinbase's separate corporate lobbying program (about \$3.88 million federally disclosed in 2024 per OpenSecrets) and the 501(c)(4) advocacy vehicle Stand With Crypto, this infrastructure altered the median position of the incoming Senate Banking Committee and the incoming administration on digital-asset policy. Executive Order 14405, signed 6 March 2025, establishing the Strategic Bitcoin Reserve and Digital Asset Stockpile, is the first administrative artifact that lies plausibly downstream of these election outcomes (Federal Register, 11 March 2025).

## Fairshake PAC architecture

### Founding and structure

Fairshake registered with the FEC on 23 March 2023 as a hybrid super PAC (independent-expenditure-only committee with a non-contribution account), FEC committee ID **C00835959**. It was seeded by Coinbase, Ripple, and the Andreessen Horowitz crypto fund (a16z) explicitly to function as the industry's electoral spending vehicle for the 2024 cycle. The structure is unusual: rather than running one cross-party committee, the founders set up two satellite super PACs that receive the bulk of their money as intra-committee transfers from Fairshake, which lets them brand independent expenditures with partisan-coded names without diluting the underlying donor pool.

### Affiliated PACs

**Defend American Jobs**, FEC ID **C00836221**, registered 29 March 2023, supports Republican candidates. Its 2024 spending was substantially funded by transfers from Fairshake.

**Protect Progress**, FEC ID **C00848440**, registered 18 August 2023, supports Democratic candidates. Same transfer pattern: in the partial 2026-cycle figures publicly visible, \$11 million of \$13.2 million in receipts came as a single transfer from Fairshake (Follow the Crypto, accessed May 2026).

### Cycle-total spend

Fairshake reported **\$260,070,709** raised and **\$195,826,511** spent during the 2023-2024 cycle (OpenSecrets PAC profile; FEC committee page). At cycle end the committee held more than \$100 million in cash, which it has carried into the 2026 cycle.

## Specific race deep-dives

### OH-Senate: Brown vs. Moreno

Sherrod Brown chaired the Senate Banking Committee for the 117th and 118th Congresses and was the principal Senate-side blocker on stablecoin legislation favored by Coinbase and Circle. His committee did not advance the Clarity for Payment Stablecoins Act in 2023 or 2024 despite House passage of related text. Fairshake and Defend American Jobs spent more than \$40 million against Brown, more than three times the \$12 million Fairshake initially announced when it entered the race (DL News, 6 November 2024; CoinDesk, 26 September 2024). Bernie Moreno is a Cleveland-area car dealer who founded Champ Titles, a blockchain car-title startup. He defeated Brown 50.6 to 46.0. The 2024 Ohio Senate race was the most expensive Senate race in U.S. history at the time, with roughly \$483 million in total spending across all committees (multiple outlets). Brown's loss removed the single most important institutional veto point on industry-favored stablecoin and market-structure legislation.

### CA-Senate primary: Porter vs. Schiff

Fairshake spent approximately \$10 million in independent expenditures opposing Representative Katie Porter, one of the Senate Banking Committee's allies on consumer-finance issues, in the March 2024 California Senate primary. Spending escalated from \$2.9 million in mid-February to roughly \$8.9 million by early March 2024, focused on a TV spot that the Sacramento Bee fact-checked as "mostly false" (HuffPost; Cointelegraph). Adam Schiff advanced; Porter was eliminated from Congress entirely. This is the cleanest causal-inference case in the cycle, in the sense that Fairshake intervened in a Democrat-on-Democrat primary in which the opposing candidate (Schiff) had not taken strong crypto positions either way.

### TX-Senate: Cruz vs. Allred

The crypto PAC complex spent comparatively modestly in Texas, given Cruz's solid early polling. Late in the cycle, as polling tightened, the Bitcoin Freedom PAC routed \$350,000 to Truth and Courage PAC explicitly to support Cruz, and Fairshake-affiliated spending picked up (The Intercept, 25 October 2024). Cruz held his seat. The Cruz race is the cleanest example of the industry hedging late in a competitive race where the favored candidate had already taken pro-industry positions.

### NY-16 primary: Bowman vs. Latimer

Fairshake spent \$2.08 million against Representative Jamaal Bowman starting 14 June 2024. The race was dominated by AIPAC's United Democracy Project at \$14.5 million, the largest single-race House primary spend on record at the time (Truthout, June 2024). Fairshake's intervention is most useful here as evidence that the PAC was willing to operate as a junior partner in coalition spending against incumbents whose general policy posture, not just crypto position, the industry disliked.

### MT-Senate: Tester vs. Sheehy

Jon Tester, a Banking Committee Democrat who had publicly said crypto "did not pass the smell test" in 2022, lost to Tim Sheehy, who described crypto as "the future of finance." Fairshake-complex spending against Tester was a major component of the roughly \$300 million spent in Montana, though I have not been able to isolate Fairshake's share precisely from public FEC summaries available at compile time.

### MD-Senate: a hedge that worked either way

Fairshake spent against David Trone in the Maryland Democratic primary, where Trone had clashed with the industry. Angela Alsobrooks won the primary and the general against Larry Hogan. The industry got a Senate Democrat without an established anti-crypto record.

## Stand With Crypto Alliance

### Founding by Coinbase

Stand With Crypto Alliance was launched 14 August 2023 by Faryar Shirzad, Coinbase's chief policy officer, as a 501(c)(4) social-welfare organization (Coinbase blog, 14 August 2023; CNN Business, 14 August 2023). The 501(c)(4) form means the entity is permitted to conduct unlimited issue advocacy and a limited amount of express political activity without disclosing donors. Coinbase has acknowledged in 10-K and policy communications that it is the principal funder, alongside Ripple, a16z, OpenSea, and Kraken-affiliated giving (CoinDesk, 31 October 2023, reported initial \$2M from 80,000 supporters plus institutional seed).

### Membership claims and verification

The organization claimed more than **443,000 advocates** as of May 2024 and "more than 2 million members" in later 2024 communications. The 2 million figure has not been independently audited and is best treated as self-reported. The 443,000 figure aligns with the 52,000 first-day signups reported on launch and a roughly nine-month growth trajectory.

### 2024 advocacy activities

Stand With Crypto's core 2024 product was a candidate scorecard rating federal candidates A through F on crypto policy stances. Donald Trump received an A; Kamala Harris received no rating during the campaign and was later rated B-minus after she signaled openness to a "responsible crypto framework" in September 2024. The organization conducted town halls, a presidential-candidate forum, and digital voter-mobilization at scale. It reports having raised, across affiliated entities and parent Coinbase-coordinated spending, more than **\$86 million** for 2024 advocacy operations (CoinDesk, 10 May 2024). This figure includes amounts routed to Fairshake and so should not be added to Fairshake's totals without netting.

## Coinbase corporate lobbying

### Annual spend trajectory

OpenSecrets reports the following Coinbase federal lobbying totals (LDA disclosures, by calendar year):

| Year | Disclosed federal lobby spend |
|---|---|
| 2021 | ~\$1.5M |
| 2022 | ~\$3.3M |
| 2023 | ~\$2.5M |
| 2024 | ~\$3.88M |

OpenSecrets cautions that the 2024 figure reflects Q1-Q3 disclosures available at retrieval; the full-year number may be modestly higher.

### Top lobbying firms retained (2024)

Per LDA filings indexed at lda.senate.gov: in-house Coinbase Government Relations staff plus retained firms including Brownstein Hyatt Farber Schreck, Franklin Square Group, and Klein/Johnson Group. Specific dollar allocations per firm are itemized in the underlying LD-2 filings; this dossier does not enumerate them.

### Issues lobbied

LD-2 issue codes filed by Coinbase in 2024 cluster in three areas:

1. **Stablecoin legislation**: the Clarity for Payment Stablecoins Act and Senate companion text, including the Lummis-Gillibrand framework.
2. **Market structure**: the Financial Innovation and Technology for the 21st Century Act (FIT21), which passed the House 21 May 2024 with a substantial Democratic minority vote.
3. **Tax treatment**: opposing the IRS digital-asset broker rule and the proposed Section 6045 expansion.

A secondary cluster covers SEC enforcement reform, in particular language responding to the Coinbase v. SEC litigation.

## Trump 2024 campaign and crypto industry

### Mar-a-Lago, May-June 2024

Trump pivoted from a 2021 stance that bitcoin "seems like a scam" to an explicit industry alliance over a series of meetings beginning at Mar-a-Lago in May 2024 with industry backers, followed by a David Sacks-hosted high-dollar San Francisco fundraiser in June 2024 and a separate Mar-a-Lago meeting with bitcoin miners later that month (PBS NewsHour; Washington Post). Press reporting at the time described commitments from attendees to support Trump's campaign financially in exchange for specific policy posture changes.

### Bitcoin 2024 Nashville, 27 July 2024

Trump delivered the keynote at the Bitcoin 2024 conference at Music City Center in Nashville on 27 July 2024. He made four commitments in the speech (CNBC, NBC News, Bitcoin Magazine):

1. The U.S. government would never sell its existing bitcoin holdings.
2. He would fire SEC Chair Gary Gensler "on day one."
3. He would create a "bitcoin and crypto presidential advisory council."
4. He endorsed (without formally adopting) Senator Cynthia Lummis's proposal for a federal reserve to acquire one million bitcoin over five years.

These commitments map almost directly onto the operative provisions of Executive Order 14405.

### Direct industry contributions to Trump

Industry contributions specifically to Trump-aligned vehicles (NBC News, 16 September 2024; Cointelegraph): the Trump 47 Committee received roughly \$1.7 million in bitcoin from Cameron and Tyler Winklevoss combined; the Make America Great Again PAC received over \$700,000 from the same donors; America PAC received \$250,000 each from the two. The Trump campaign reported \$3 million in crypto donations in Q2 2024 (Cointelegraph) and the Trump-aligned PAC complex reported \$7.5 million in crypto from early June through mid-October 2024 (CNBC, 15 October 2024).

Total identifiable crypto-industry political spending in the 2024 cycle, including Fairshake's \$260 million in receipts, direct candidate contributions, and Trump-vehicle giving, reached approximately **\$190 million in independent expenditures plus tens of millions in direct contributions** (NBC News, 16 September 2024; The New Republic, August 2024).

## Causal chain to EO 14405

The traceable path runs through five identifiable steps:

1. **Pre-cycle infrastructure.** Coinbase, Ripple, and a16z capitalize Fairshake (March 2023) and Stand With Crypto (August 2023), creating both the spending vehicle and the issue-advocacy 501(c)(4).
2. **Primary intervention.** Fairshake spends \$10 million removing Katie Porter from the California Senate primary and \$2 million as a junior partner in defeating Jamaal Bowman in NY-16. Both signals are received by other House and Senate Democrats.
3. **Candidate alignment.** Trump's policy posture shifts at Mar-a-Lago in May 2024 and is formalized at Bitcoin 2024 in Nashville on 27 July 2024, with four explicit commitments mapping to later EO text.
4. **General-election removal of veto points.** Fairshake and Defend American Jobs spend ~\$40 million to defeat Sherrod Brown, removing the Senate Banking chair who had blocked stablecoin legislation, and contribute to the defeat of Jon Tester. Of 58 races, 53 industry-preferred candidates win.
5. **Administrative payoff.** Trump nominates pro-industry personnel (Paul Atkins to SEC, David Sacks as crypto and AI czar, Brian Quintenz to CFTC) and signs **Executive Order 14405** on 6 March 2025, establishing the Strategic Bitcoin Reserve and U.S. Digital Asset Stockpile (Federal Register, 11 March 2025; White House Fact Sheet, 6 March 2025).

The chain is dense but not deterministic. Brown's loss in Ohio is plausibly attributable to multiple factors including the top of the ticket. Porter's loss in California involved factors beyond Fairshake's spending. What is documentable is that the industry built a spending machine sized to a small number of pivotal races, won the large majority of them, and obtained an executive order that translates its stated policy preferences into administrative reality within ten weeks of inauguration.

## Uncertainty

1. Race-by-race spend attribution for the Tester-Sheehy Montana Senate race is not isolated cleanly in public summaries available at compile time. The aggregate \$40M-plus figure circulating in press reports may be conflating Fairshake spending with other Republican-aligned outside groups; verification against the FEC F3X disbursement schedules for C00835959 and C00836221 is needed before any single-race figure is cited in the paper.
2. The Coinbase 2024 lobbying total of \$3.88M reflects Q1-Q3 disclosures available at retrieval. The full-year LDA total may be modestly higher. The figure also excludes spending by Coinbase-affiliated 501(c)(4) entities (notably Stand With Crypto), which are not LDA-reportable.
3. Stand With Crypto's "2 million members" claim is self-reported and unaudited. The 443,000 figure (May 2024) is consistent with public growth reporting and is the more defensible number.
4. The \$86 million figure for Stand With Crypto-affiliated 2024 advocacy includes amounts routed to Fairshake. It should not be added to Fairshake's \$260M receipts without netting, or the same dollars get counted twice.
5. The causal claim from election outcomes to EO 14405 is supported by direct correspondence between Trump's 27 July 2024 Nashville commitments and EO 14405's operative provisions, but cannot be reduced to a single-instrument identification. The paper should treat it as a documented sequence rather than as an identified effect.
6. Several donor-level figures cited in trade press (for example, specific amounts from Jesse Powell, Brad Garlinghouse, and Jeremy Allaire personally) require FEC Form 3X line-item verification before being cited in the working paper. The Coinbase, Ripple, and a16z aggregate totals are well-attested; individual founder-level breakdowns are less so.
