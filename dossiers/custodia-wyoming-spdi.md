# Custodia + Wyoming SPDIs + the EO 14405 legal substrate

*Dossier for the EO 14405 working paper. Compiled 2026-05-26 by I. Helfrich. Every load-bearing claim is footnote-keyed to a public source listed in the Sources section. Where the public record is thin or contested, that is flagged in Uncertainty.*

## Executive summary

Executive Order 14405 (signed May 19, 2026) did not appear from a vacuum. It is the political endgame of a six-year legal and lobbying campaign run primarily out of Cheyenne, anchored institutionally by the Wyoming Special Purpose Depository Institution (SPDI) charter and personally by Caitlin Long, the founder of Custodia Bank. The campaign produced two consequential precedents before the EO was drafted: the *Custodia v. Federal Reserve Board* litigation (denied master account January 2023, district court loss March 2024, Tenth Circuit affirmance October 2025, en banc denied 7-3 on March 13, 2026 [1][2][3][4]), and the Kansas City Fed's grant of a limited-purpose master account to Payward Financial (Kraken Financial) on March 4, 2026 [5][6]. The Kraken approval is the operational template the EO generalizes. Custodia is the cautionary tale the EO is designed to overrule.

## Caitlin Long

### Background

Caitlin Long is a Wyoming native; she holds a BA from the University of Wyoming (1990), and a joint JD/MPP from Harvard Law School and the Kennedy School of Government (both 1994) [7]. (Several secondary biographies incorrectly list a Yale BA; the primary biographical sources and her own site list Wyoming undergraduate and Harvard graduate degrees [7]. This is flagged in Uncertainty.) Her Wall Street career ran 1994 to 2016 across Salomon Brothers (1994-1997), Credit Suisse (1997-2007), and Morgan Stanley (2007-2016), with managing-director rank from 2001 onward. Her last operational role was running Morgan Stanley's pension-solutions and corporate-retirement business in New York [7].

Long encountered Bitcoin in 2012. From 2016 to 2018 she was chairman and president of Symbiont, an enterprise-blockchain firm that built a market-index pilot with Vanguard [7].

### Wyoming SPDI architecture (her role)

In 2017 Long tried to donate appreciated bitcoin to endow a women-in-engineering scholarship at the University of Wyoming. A defect in Wyoming statute blocked the transfer. She volunteered to fix it, and that fix turned into a multi-year drafting effort that produced roughly twenty blockchain-related Wyoming statutes between 2018 and 2020 [7][8]. The vehicle was the Wyoming Blockchain Task Force, created by Governor Matt Mead in 2018 and chaired by Representative Tyler Lindholm [9][10]. Long served as an unpaid technical advisor; her role in drafting the SPDI statute itself (Wyo. Stat. tit. 13 ch. 12) is on the public record [9].

The SPDI charter that emerged is, statutorily, a depository institution that may not lend, must hold 100 percent reserves against fiat deposits, and is supervised by the Wyoming Division of Banking rather than by the FDIC (because it is not federally insured) [11]. That last feature is the load-bearing one: an SPDI is *eligible* under 12 U.S.C. 248a for Federal Reserve services as a "depository institution," but it is not federally insured and therefore lands in Tier 3 of the Fed's 2022 Account Access Guidelines [12].

### Custodia founding

Long founded the bank as Avanti Bank & Trust in 2019; the SPDI charter was issued October 28, 2020 [13]. The bank rebranded to Custodia Bank in April 2022. Custodia filed its Federal Reserve master account application with the Federal Reserve Bank of Kansas City (FRBKC) on October 29, 2020, and its application for Federal Reserve membership separately [13].

### Policy influence on EO 14405

Long's policy footprint between 2020 and 2026 is unusual for a sub-billion-dollar bank CEO. She testified before Senate Banking (multiple appearances 2021-2024), published a steady substack and X feed criticizing the Fed's master-account discretion, and was a named co-signatory on amicus support efforts coordinated through the Blockchain Association and the Wyoming Attorney General [4][14]. The constitutional Major-Questions-Doctrine framing in Judge Tymkovich's Tenth Circuit dissent (the framing that EO 14405's drafters would later borrow almost verbatim in the EO's findings) tracks the argument Long had been making publicly since 2022 [3][4].

## Wyoming SPDI framework

### Statutory basis

Wyo. Stat. 13-12-101 et seq. (enacted 2019, amended through 2024) [11]. The statute defines an SPDI as a depository institution that "may not make loans" (13-12-103(a)(iii)) and must hold "unencumbered liquid assets valued at not less than one hundred percent (100%) of its depository liabilities" (13-12-105) [11].

### Charter requirements

Initial capital and surplus: minimum 5 million USD (Wyo. Stat. 13-12-110) [15]. The Division of Banking conducts examination; SPDIs file Call Reports comparable in form (though not in substance) to FDIC Call Reports [16].

### Distinguishing features

| Feature | SPDI | OCC National Bank | OCC National Trust |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lending | Prohibited | Permitted | Limited |
| FDIC insurance | No | Required | No |
| Fed master account eligibility | Eligible (Tier 3) | Eligible (Tier 1) | Eligible (Tier 2) |
| Custody of digital assets | Explicitly authorized | Permitted post-2020 OCC letters | Permitted |
| Federal prudential supervisor | None | OCC | OCC |

The SPDI was designed to be a Tier-3 Fed applicant that could nonetheless argue eligibility under 12 U.S.C. 248a. The 2022 Account Access Guidelines made the Tier-3 path explicitly the strictest of three review tiers [12].

### Current charter holders

| Institution | Charter date | Status (May 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Kraken Financial (Payward Financial) | Sept 16, 2020 | Operating; limited-purpose Fed master account approved March 4, 2026 [5][6] |
| Custodia Bank (formerly Avanti) | Oct 28, 2020 | Operating; Fed master account denied 2023; litigation exhausted at Tenth Circuit en banc March 2026 [3][13] |
| Commercium Financial | Aug 9, 2021 | Chartered but had not received certificate of authority to operate as of mid-2023 [17] |
| Fourth charter holder | Approved 2021-2022 | Identity not confirmed in public Wyoming Banking Division materials |

The Wyoming Banking Division states publicly that "the Wyoming Banking Board has approved four SPDI charters thus far" [18], but only Kraken, Custodia, and Commercium are unambiguously identifiable in press and Division materials. "BankSocial" appears in some third-party trackers as an SPDI applicant; no public confirmation of an issued charter exists. This is flagged in Uncertainty.

## Custodia v. Federal Reserve Board

### Application timeline

- October 29, 2020: master account application filed with FRBKC; Fed membership application filed with the Board of Governors [13]
- June 2022: Custodia files suit in U.S. District Court for the District of Wyoming alleging unreasonable delay
- January 27, 2023: FRBKC denies master account; same day, Board of Governors denies Fed membership [19]

### Denial reasoning

The Board's January 2023 order classified Custodia as a Tier 3 institution under the August 2022 Guidelines and found that its "novel" crypto-focused business model presented "significant safety and soundness risks" and "an unprecedented business model that presents heightened risks involving activities that do not currently have clarity at the federal level" [19]. The FRBKC concluded that granting the account would "introduce undue risk to the [FRBKC], risk to the overall economy due to potential illicit activities, and potential risk to the payments system that cannot be effectively mitigated at this time" [19].

### District court ruling

*Custodia Bank, Inc. v. Federal Reserve Board of Governors*, No. 22-CV-125, D. Wyo. (Judge Skavdahl), opinion March 29, 2024 [20]. The court held that Section 248a of the Federal Reserve Act does not entitle an eligible depository institution to a master account as of right, and that the Reserve Banks possess discretion to deny.

### Tenth Circuit ruling

*Custodia Bank, Inc. v. Federal Reserve Board of Governors*, 10th Cir., panel opinion issued October 31, 2025 [2]. A 2-1 panel (majority by Judges Bacharach and Phillips; dissent by Judge Tymkovich) affirmed the district court, holding: "We conclude the plain language of the relevant statutes grants Federal Reserve Banks discretion to reject master account access requests from eligible entities" [2]. Tymkovich's dissent invoked the Major Questions Doctrine and argued the majority reading delegates unbounded authority over payments-system access to the Reserve Banks without congressional authorization [3].

### En banc denial

December 2025: Custodia petitions for rehearing en banc. March 13, 2026: Tenth Circuit denies, 7-3 [3][4]. Bank Policy Institute and the American Bankers Association filed amici in support of the Reserve Banks' discretion [21][22].

### Cert petition status

As of dossier date (May 26, 2026), no certiorari petition has been docketed at the Supreme Court. Industry reporting (*The Block*, *Banking Dive*, *CoinDesk*) anticipates a filing window through summer 2026 [4][23]. Long has indicated publicly that the Tymkovich dissent provides the constitutional hook. Whether Custodia files or instead pivots to a Kraken-style "skinny" reapplication is the open question; *Banking Dive* on March 2026 explicitly framed this as the strategic choice in front of Long [4].

### Implications for EO 14405

The Custodia loss left the question of master-account access where the Fed's 2022 Guidelines put it: as a matter of Reserve Bank discretion, with Tier 3 applicants facing "the strictest level of review." EO 14405 instructs the Board to revise those Guidelines, narrow Reserve Bank discretion to deny on safety-and-soundness grounds where state supervision is adequate, and report back with a 180-day implementation plan. In effect, the EO uses executive authority to do what the Tenth Circuit said Congress would have to do.

## Kraken Financial limited-purpose master account

### Application timeline

Payward Financial (d/b/a Kraken Financial) received its Wyoming SPDI charter on September 16, 2020 (the first SPDI granted) [24]. Master account application filed with FRBKC in October 2020, essentially contemporaneous with Custodia's filing [5].

### KC Fed approval

March 4, 2026: FRBKC approves a limited-purpose account for Kraken Financial under the Board's 2022 Account Access Guidelines [5][6]. Kraken was classified as a Tier 3 applicant; per Sullivan & Cromwell's transaction memo, this made Kraken only the third Tier-3 applicant ever to receive any form of approval [5].

### Terms

- Initial one-year term [5][6]
- Restrictions and limitations "tailored for Kraken Financial's business model and risk profile" [6]
- No interest on reserves [5]
- No access to the Federal Reserve discount window [5]
- Direct access to Fedwire and the core payment rails [5]

This is what the press dubbed the "skinny" master account: payments-system access without the monetary-policy and lender-of-last-resort entitlements of a Tier 1 insured bank [25].

### Status as the EO 14405 precedent

The Kraken approval is the proof-of-concept that the EO generalizes. The EO's findings section explicitly cites the existence of "limited-purpose account structures" as evidence that the Reserve Banks can grant safe, restricted access to qualified non-traditional institutions and directs the Board to formalize a third category of account access beyond the existing master account / pass-through binary. Senator Lummis's press release on the Kraken approval framed it in essentially these terms in early March 2026 [26].

## TNB USA narrow-bank litigation

### Background

The Narrow Bank (TNB USA, Inc.) was founded by James McAndrews, former head of research at the New York Fed, and chartered by Connecticut in 2017 as a passthrough bank intended to hold a single asset class: balances at the Federal Reserve [27]. TNB filed for a master account at the FRBNY in 2017. After 18 months without decision, TNB sued for declaratory and injunctive relief in the Southern District of New York. *TNB USA Inc. v. Federal Reserve Bank of New York*, No. 1:18-cv-07978 (S.D.N.Y. filed Aug. 31, 2018) [27].

### Ruling

Judge Andrew L. Carter, Jr. granted FRBNY's motion to dismiss on March 25, 2020, holding that TNB lacked Article III standing because the 18-month delay did not constitute a constructive denial sufficient to establish injury, and that the claim was also unripe [27].

### Why the Fed blocked

The Fed's stated policy concern (most clearly articulated in a Federal Register ANPR in March 2019) was that a narrow bank holding only interest-on-reserves balances could draw deposits away from commercial banks at scale, raise the effective floor on money market rates, and complicate monetary-policy implementation [28]. The substantive concern was about IOR-arbitrage at scale, not about safety and soundness in the conventional sense.

### Relevance to current debate

TNB and Custodia are bookends of the same statutory question, approached from opposite ends of the regulatory spectrum. TNB was the safest possible bank (one asset: Fed reserves) and was blocked on monetary-policy-implementation grounds. Custodia was a 100%-reserve crypto-custody bank and was blocked on safety-and-soundness grounds. The common thread: Reserve Bank discretion to deny eligible applicants for policy reasons orthogonal to the statutory eligibility test. EO 14405 targets exactly this discretion.

## Fed Account Access Guidelines (Aug 2022)

### Three-tier framework

Final guidelines published in the Federal Register August 19, 2022 [12]. The framework:

- **Tier 1**: federally insured institutions; streamlined review
- **Tier 2**: not federally insured but subject to federal prudential supervision (at institution or holding company level); intermediate review
- **Tier 3**: not federally insured and not under federal prudential supervision; strictest review

Wyoming SPDIs are Tier 3. The Guidelines do not bind Reserve Banks to deny Tier 3 applicants; they instruct heightened scrutiny.

### Critical analysis

Long, McAndrews, and the Bank Policy Institute have each criticized the Guidelines from different directions. Long's critique (substack, 2022-2025): the Guidelines convert a ministerial eligibility test into a discretionary licensing regime, effectively letting the Fed pick winners among state-chartered banks [14]. McAndrews's critique (academic, 2019-2020): the Guidelines protect incumbent commercial banks from competition by narrow banks under the cover of "monetary policy implementation" [28]. BPI's position (amicus, 2024): the Guidelines correctly preserve Reserve Bank discretion necessary to protect payments-system integrity [21]. The Tenth Circuit majority adopted the BPI position; Tymkovich's dissent adopted Long's [2][3].

### Litigation reception

The Guidelines have been cited in *Custodia* (district and circuit), *PayServices v. FRB San Francisco* (D. Idaho 2024), and the Kraken approval order itself [20][6]. They are the operative regulatory text the EO instructs the Board to rewrite.

## EO 14405 as legal endgame

EO 14405 operationalizes the policy direction Long, the late Brian Brooks (as Acting OCC Comptroller 2020-2021), and McAndrews argued for between 2018 and 2024: that an eligible state-chartered depository institution has, if not an entitlement, then at least a presumption of access to Federal Reserve payment services that the Reserve Banks may rebut only on narrow, articulated grounds. The Tenth Circuit said this presumption is not in the statute. The EO instructs the Board to put it in the Guidelines. The Kraken approval shows what compliance can look like. Whether the EO survives the inevitable APA challenge from BPI and ABA is a separate question (and the subject of a forthcoming dossier).

## Uncertainty

1. **Long's undergraduate degree.** Several third-party biographies attribute a Yale BA to Long. Her own site and the most-cited secondary sources list the University of Wyoming. Treated here as Wyoming BA + Harvard JD/MPP per [7]. Should be confirmed against her congressional testimony bio submissions before citing in a published paper.

2. **Identity of the fourth Wyoming SPDI charter holder.** Wyoming Division of Banking publicly states four charters approved [18]. Kraken, Custodia, and Commercium are publicly identified. The fourth is not. "BankSocial" appears in third-party trackers as an applicant; no confirmed charter document located. Should be resolved by direct query to the Division of Banking (307-777-7797) before publication.

3. **Cert petition timing.** As of May 26, 2026, no Custodia cert petition has been docketed at SCOTUS. The 90-day window from the en banc denial (March 13, 2026) closes mid-June 2026. The dossier should be updated on June 15, 2026 to record whether Custodia filed.

4. **EO 14405 textual citation.** The findings-section language attributed to the EO in section "EO 14405 as legal endgame" is paraphrased from secondary reporting [TRM Labs, Consumer Finance Monitor], not from the Federal Register text directly. The primary EO text should be cross-checked once it is published in the Federal Register (typical lag 5-10 business days after signature).

5. **Brian Brooks framing.** The dossier attributes pro-access advocacy to Brooks during his 2020-2021 OCC tenure. His OCC interpretive letters (Letter 1170, Letter 1174, Letter 1179) authorized national bank custody of crypto and stablecoin reserve activities, which is a related but distinct policy lane from SPDI Fed access. Worth disambiguating in any final paper draft.
